Outcome pricing elasticity in SME AI adoption.

M. Orren · Autopilot Labs Research· A. Sarraf† · Univ. of Melbourne, Dept. of Economics· J. Chen · Autopilot Labs Research

† Corresponding author · sarraf@unimelb.edu.au
outcome-pricing sme-adoption price-elasticity ai-automation
Abstract

We study how small and mid-sized firms respond to three pricing structures for AI automation: flat seat fees, token pass-through, and per-outcome rates with SLA. Using a 14-month panel of 412 Australian SMEs onboarded through Autopilot Labs, we estimate own-price elasticities of -1.8 for per-seat contracts and -0.6 for per-outcome contracts. Results suggest outcome pricing is markedly stickier and expands the addressable market for AI services by roughly 38%. We argue that seat pricing rations AI away from the firms that would benefit most.

A natural experiment in paralegal augmentation.

Autopilot Labs Research · Autopilot Labs Research· E. Dubois† · Australian National University, School of Law· R. Nguyen · ANU, Research School of Economics

† Corresponding author · e.dubois@anu.edu.au
legal-tech labour-augmentation difference-in-differences discovery-review
Abstract

A Sydney litigation firm rolled out an AI discovery agent across six of eleven practice groups during 2025, creating a near-ideal staggered adoption design. We use a difference-in-differences model to compare paralegal throughput, error rates, and self-reported task satisfaction pre- and post-rollout. Treated paralegals handled 2.8x more documents per hour with no statistically significant change in privilege-flag error rates. Task satisfaction rose in three of six groups, fell in one, and was unchanged in two.

SLA-driven contracts vs. time-and-materials: a field study.

J. Chen · Autopilot Labs Research· P. Iyer† · UNSW Business School, School of Management· D. Halligan · Autopilot Labs Research

† Corresponding author · p.iyer@unsw.edu.au
contract-theory sla services-procurement ai-services
Abstract

We compare the economic outcomes of 38 AI automation engagements billed under SLA-driven per-outcome contracts against a matched sample of 41 engagements billed on time-and-materials terms. SLA contracts delivered workflows to production 11 days earlier on median and showed a 22% lower rate of scope disputes. Buyers of SLA contracts reported fewer renegotiations but slightly narrower feature creep. We discuss implications for AI services procurement and suggest a "reference SLA" template for practitioners.

Hallucination rates across eight vertical agents.

D. Halligan · Autopilot Labs Research· S. Okonjo · Autopilot Labs Research· K. Trifonov† · UNSW, School of Computer Science

† Corresponding author · k.trifonov@unsw.edu.au
ai-safety hallucination benchmarks vertical-agents
Abstract

We measure factual error rates — "hallucinations" — across Autopilot Labs's eight production vertical agents (healthcare, legal, finance, real estate, e-commerce, HR, marketing, manufacturing) over 3.4 million production outcomes. Using human-adjudicated ground truth on a 12,000-outcome sample, we report per-vertical error rates ranging from 0.09% (finance) to 1.4% (marketing). We document a strong, monotonic relationship between narrower task scope and lower error rate. Results support domain-specific agent design over general-purpose systems for production AI automation.

Workflow audit ROI: predicting automatable vs. not.

R. Nguyen† · ANU, Research School of Economics· M. Orren · Autopilot Labs Research

† Corresponding author · r.nguyen@anu.edu.au
workflow-audit task-automation predictive-model roi
Abstract

From 214 completed Autopilot Labs workflow audits, we hand-label 3,118 candidate sub-tasks as automated, partially automated, or rejected for automation. We train a lightweight classifier — 9 hand-engineered features, no model fine-tuning — that predicts automation feasibility with 84% accuracy on a held-out sample. Feature importance is dominated by three signals: task repetition, variance in input format, and tolerance for minor errors. We publish the audit rubric and encourage practitioners to calibrate their own thresholds.

Per-outcome pricing and labour market effects.

A. Sarraf† · Univ. of Melbourne, Dept. of Economics· E. Dubois · ANU, School of Law· M. Orren · Autopilot Labs Research

† Corresponding author · sarraf@unimelb.edu.au
labour-economics wage-effects task-displacement outcome-pricing
Abstract

We study wage and employment effects in 28 client firms that transitioned from headcount-based AI services to per-outcome pricing between 2024 and 2026. Contrary to a naive displacement hypothesis, mean headcount in affected teams was approximately stable, while the mix of work shifted sharply: time on rote task execution fell by 61% while time on exception handling, client-facing work, and QA rose correspondingly. Real wages for retained workers rose 3.1% above sector baseline. We interpret the results cautiously, noting selection effects and a relatively short panel.

§ 03 — Submit research · 2026 call

For academic researchers working on AI automation.

We open two research slots per year to independent academics whose work touches the economics, labour, safety, or operations of AI automation. Selected collaborators receive privileged data access, named authorship, and a small-but-honest research stipend. No deliverable is required beyond a circulating paper.
