No. 007 — Plain-English glossary

Plain-English glossary.

Everything Autopilot Labs means when we talk about automating work with AI. No marketing words. No jargon that only makes sense to the vendor. Just the terms we actually use inside the ops console, in the contract, and on the audit report.

37 terms · A–Z
A 3 entries

Agent

Noun · Platform

A single piece of software that Autopilot Labs builds to finish one job — a triaged claim, a tagged lease, a posted journal entry. An agent is how we automate with AI the parts of a workflow a person used to do by hand, end to end, without needing a human to click through it.

Audit

Noun · Process

Our free two-week look at how your team actually works. Two Autopilot Labs engineers sit with your people, map the manual path end to end, and come back with a written list of jobs we think we can automate — and the ones we can't. No audit, no build.

Automation

Noun · Concept

When a job that used to be done by a person is now done by software without the person in the middle. Autopilot Labs-style automation always uses AI for the messy parts — reading a fax, matching a vendor, deciding which bucket an email belongs in — not just rules-based scripts.

B 1 entry

Baseline

Noun · Measurement

What the work costs and how long it takes before we ship an agent — measured in hours, dollars per outcome, and error rate. We record the baseline during the audit so there's an honest number to compare against once the agent is running.

C 1 entry

Credit-back

Noun · Billing

If an agent misses its SLA on an outcome, you don't pay for that outcome — and the miss is credited back to your invoice automatically. No disputes, no emails, no quarterly true-ups. The ops console shows the credit in the same row as the missed job.

D 2 entries

Diagnostic

Noun · Process

The written report we hand you at the end of the free audit. Lists every workflow we observed, the manual hours it eats per week, the rough cost per outcome, and a plain-English opinion on whether AI can do it. You keep the diagnostic whether or not we end up working together.

Deploy

Verb · Process

The day an agent moves out of shadow mode and starts being paid for its outcomes. Deploy usually happens around day 14 of an engagement, after the agent has shadowed the human team long enough that its outputs match theirs.

E 1 entry

EMR

Noun · Healthcare

Electronic Medical Record — the system a clinic uses to store patient charts (Epic, Cerner, Best Practice, Medical Director). Our healthcare agents read from and write to your EMR over the same APIs your staff use, so no one is copying data between tabs.

F 1 entry

Fail-safe

Noun · Platform

The rule an agent follows when it isn't confident enough to finish a job on its own — usually "pause and hand to a named human." Every Autopilot Labs agent ships with a fail-safe threshold; below that confidence score, the work quietly lands in a human queue instead of going out the door wrong.

G 1 entry

Gatekeeper

Noun · Platform

A small rules-only check that sits in front of every AI call — it enforces policy, masks PII, and blocks anything the contract says the agent is not allowed to do. The gatekeeper is deterministic software, not AI, and the same check runs in every environment.

H 2 entries

Hallucination

Noun · Risk

When an AI model confidently states something that isn't true — a fake case citation, a made-up policy number, an invented vendor. We treat every agent output as suspect by default and cross-check load-bearing facts against your own data before anything ships.

Human-in-the-loop

Noun · Workflow

A workflow where the AI does the heavy lifting but a person reviews the edge cases before anything goes out. Most Autopilot Labs agents start human-in-the-loop for the first month, then drop out of the loop as the confidence numbers stabilise.

I 3 entries

Inference

Noun · Platform

A single call to the AI model — you ask it a question, it answers. Inference is where the underlying token cost lives. You never see those numbers on an Autopilot Labs invoice because we price the outcome, not the inference.

Intake

Noun · Workflow

The moment messy information enters a business — a fax, an email with an attachment, a PDF form, a voicemail. Intake agents clean it up and push a structured record into your system so everyone downstream has tidy data to work with.

Integration

Noun · Platform

A connection between an Autopilot Labs agent and a system your team already uses — Salesforce, Xero, Epic, Relativity, whatever. We build and maintain every integration; it's part of the per-outcome price, not a line item billed separately.

J 1 entry

Jurisdiction

Noun · Compliance

The country (and sometimes state) whose laws apply to your data and to the work an agent does. Autopilot Labs pins each agent to a jurisdiction — Australia, EU, UK, US, Singapore — and the gatekeeper blocks anything that would cross the line.

K 1 entry

Kill-switch

Noun · Platform

One button in the ops console that stops an agent in its tracks. Everything in flight pauses, the human queue picks up the work, and no new outcomes are billed. Every client has a named owner for the kill-switch.

L 2 entries

Latency

Noun · SLA

How long the agent takes from the moment a job lands until the outcome ships. Written into every SLA as a P95 number — "95% of outcomes complete in under X seconds" — not a misleading average. Beat it, we get paid. Miss it, credit-back.

Loop

Noun · Workflow

A repetitive chunk of work your team does over and over — claims, tickets, leases, briefs, CVs, invoices. Loops are what we automate with AI; one-offs aren't worth the engineering. If it happens 200+ times a month, it's a loop worth pricing.

M 1 entry

Median SLA

Noun · Billing

The number in the middle when you line up every SLA result from the month — half were faster, half were slower. We report median alongside P95 because averages hide outliers and averages get gamed. Median is honest.

N 1 entry

NCR

Noun · Manufacturing

Non-Conformance Report — the paperwork a factory fills out when a part fails QA. Our manufacturing agent drafts the NCR from the inspection photos and sensor logs, then routes it to the quality engineer for a 15-second review instead of a 45-minute write-up.

O 1 entry

Outcome

Noun · Billing

One finished unit of work — a posted journal entry, a closed support ticket, a drafted prior-auth, a tagged discovery document. Outcomes are the only thing Autopilot Labs bills for. If the agent didn't finish the job to SLA, it wasn't an outcome.

P 2 entries

Per-outcome pricing

Term · Billing

You pay a fixed rate for each finished, SLA-passing unit of work. Not per seat, not per token, not per month. This is the way we think you should buy AI services — we only get paid when the agent actually does the job — and it's how most of our clients are priced.

Prior-auth

Noun · Healthcare

A prior authorisation — the form a clinician has to fill out to get an insurer to approve a treatment before it happens. Prior-auth is one of the most punishing workflows in healthcare, and one of the first places teams ask us to automate with AI.

Q 1 entry

Queue

Noun · Platform

The list of jobs waiting to be finished. Autopilot Labs operates two queues per workflow — an agent queue (handled automatically) and a human queue (edge cases and fail-safes). Both are visible to you live in the ops console, with age and priority on each job.

R 2 entries

Redaction

Noun · Compliance

Replacing sensitive fields — names, Medicare numbers, bank accounts — with placeholders before any text is sent to the AI model. Redaction happens at the gatekeeper, every single time, and the original never leaves your jurisdiction.

Residency

Noun · Compliance

Where your data physically lives while the agent is working on it. Autopilot Labs supports AU, EU, UK, US, and SG residency; you pick one per agent, and the data never crosses out of that region — not even briefly for an AI call.

S 3 entries

Seat

Noun · Billing

A user account on the Autopilot Labs ops console. Seats are free and unlimited — we don't believe in seat pricing, because the work happens whether or not anyone is watching it. Every team member who wants read-only access gets one.

SLA

Noun · Billing

Service Level Agreement — the written promise about how fast and how often the agent will finish its outcomes correctly. Every Autopilot Labs contract has two SLA numbers: latency (how quickly) and reliability (what share pass). Miss either, credit-back.

Shadow mode

Noun · Workflow

Running a new agent in parallel with your human team without shipping its output — we compare drafts, measure accuracy, and tune the fail-safe before any real outcome goes out the door. Shadow mode lasts at least 5 business days on every new workflow.

T 3 entries

Token

Noun · Platform

The chunks an AI model reads and writes in — about three-quarters of a word each. Token pricing is how most AI vendors bill, and it's famously unpredictable. Tokens are Autopilot Labs's cost, not yours. You pay per outcome; we eat the token bill.

Throughput

Noun · SLA

How many outcomes an agent can finish per unit of time — per minute, per hour, per day. Throughput is how we size an agent's infrastructure and how you plan for peaks. Contracts name a guaranteed floor; we almost always beat it.

Tenant

Noun · Platform

A client's fully isolated slice of the Autopilot Labs platform — its own data, its own model prompts, its own audit log. One tenant per company, no cross-tenant training, no cross-tenant reads. Tenants can't see each other's work, ever.

V 1 entry

Volume tier

Noun · Billing

A stepped discount on per-outcome pricing — the more outcomes you ship each month, the lower the rate per outcome. Volume tiers are written into the contract, not "talk to sales." You can see your current tier and the next threshold live in the ops console.

W 2 entries

Warm path

Noun · Platform

A pre-loaded agent that's ready to run instantly instead of spinning up from cold. Warm paths cut first-job latency from ~8 seconds to under 400ms — important for interactive workflows like chat, support triage, or live listings. Base plans include a starting pool of warm-path credits.

Workflow

Noun · Concept

A named, repeated piece of work inside your business — "prior-auth intake", "month-end reconciliation", "CV shortlisting". A workflow is the unit we automate with AI, audit against, price, and SLA. One workflow usually maps to one agent, sometimes two.

Y 1 entry

Yield

Noun · Measurement

The share of incoming jobs an agent finishes on its own without the human queue picking them up. High yield means the agent is confidently handling most cases; low yield means the fail-safe is doing a lot of work, which is a signal to retune.

Z 1 entry

Zero-retention

Noun · Compliance

A contract clause saying the AI model provider is not allowed to keep, log, or train on your data — not even briefly for abuse monitoring. Every Autopilot Labs agent runs on zero-retention endpoints by default; it's in the master services agreement, not an add-on.
