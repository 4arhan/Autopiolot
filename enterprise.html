Data migration

Move everything. Lose nothing.

Migrations fail quietly — a field truncated here, a duplicate there, discovered months later. We treat migration as an accounting exercise: every record that leaves the old system must arrive in the new one, and we show you the proof.

  • Profile. We scan the source data and report its real quality — gaps, duplicates and surprises — before anything moves.
  • Map and clean. Old fields are mapped to the new structure, with cleaning rules you approve.
  • Rehearse. Full practice runs on a copy of your data, so cutover day holds no surprises.
  • Reconcile. Automated counts, totals and record-by-record checks confirm source and target match.
  • Cut over. A planned switch in a window that suits you, with rollback ready.

AI security hardening

AI brings new attacks. Close them before launch.

An AI system can be talked into leaking data or taking actions it shouldn't — no malware required. Traditional security testing doesn't look for this. We do, on the AI you've built, bought or are about to switch on.

  • Attack testing. We try to manipulate your AI the way a real attacker would — hidden instructions, poisoned documents, data extraction.
  • Least-privilege agents. Every agent gets only the access its job needs, with approval steps on high-risk actions.
  • Data leak controls. Sensitive data is filtered before it reaches a model and before any answer leaves.
  • Full audit trail. Every decision and action is logged, so you can answer "why did it do that?"
  • Governance that maps to the rules. Controls aligned to NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act.

Quantum readiness

Data stolen today can be unlocked tomorrow.

The encryption protecting almost everything online — RSA and elliptic-curve — can be broken by a large enough quantum computer. Attackers know this, so they copy encrypted data now and wait. If your data needs to stay secret for ten years, the risk has already started.

2024

New standards published

NIST finalised the first post-quantum encryption standards: ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA (FIPS 203, 204, 205).

2030

Old algorithms deprecated

NIST's transition plan marks today's common RSA and elliptic-curve strengths as deprecated after 2030.

2035

Old algorithms disallowed

The same plan disallows them entirely after 2035. Large migrations take years — which is why the work starts now.
