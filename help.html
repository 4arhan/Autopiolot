Getting started

12 answers
Q 01.01 How do I start automating a workflow with AI?

Book a free workflow audit on the contact page. We spend ten working days inside your team, map the manual path end to end, and come back with a scoped proposal to automate with AI the parts where agents will actually pay for themselves.

If we can't find a workflow worth automating, you get the diagnostic on us and we part on good terms.

Q 01.02 How long does it take to go from audit to live AI agents?

Median time from signed contract to a production agent is 14 days. Simple workflows ship in a week; cross-system ones with bespoke integrations take three to four. We always run a shadow week where the agent works alongside your team before flipping to live.

Q 01.03 Do I need an internal AI or engineering team to work with Autopilot Labs?

No. Most of our clients have zero AI staff — that's why they hire us. You need one process owner per workflow (the person who knows how the work gets done today) and someone with admin rights to the systems we're connecting. We bring the AI automation engineers.

Q 01.04 What does a "workflow audit" actually involve?

Two weeks, three deliverables. One of our operators shadows your team (on-site or Zoom — your call), measures every step of the target workflow, and writes it up. You get:

  • A process map with timings per step
  • A heat map of automation candidates
  • A fixed-price quote for the shippable parts
Q 01.05 Which industries do you support?

Exactly eight, and nothing else: healthcare, legal, finance, real estate, e-commerce, HR, marketing, and manufacturing. Each vertical has a pre-built catalogue of agents, a reference SLA, and a team that already speaks your jargon.

If you're outside those eight, we'll tell you honestly on the first call.

Q 01.06 Can I try it with a small pilot before committing?

Yes — that is the default. The first 30 days are a capped pilot on one workflow. No long-term contract, no minimum spend. If the AI automation doesn't hit the agreed outcome volume or SLA, you walk away and we don't charge for the pilot outcomes.

Q 01.07 What's the minimum volume to make this worthwhile?

Roughly 100 outcomes a month is where the maths starts to work. Below that the implementation cost outruns the savings. Above, it pays back quickly — our median client sees breakeven at six weeks.

Q 01.08 Who writes the prompts and maintains the agents?

We do, end to end. Prompt engineering, evaluation sets, retraining, model upgrades, regression tests — all included in the per-outcome rate. You never touch a prompt unless you want to.

Q 01.09 How do I know which workflow to automate first?

Pick the one your team complains about on a Monday. Repetitive, high-volume, rules-driven work is where AI automation wins fastest. During the audit we rank candidates on volume, judgement required, and data quality — and start with the highest-confidence one.

Q 01.10 Do I have to sign a long contract?

No. Every plan is month-to-month with 30 days' notice. The whole point of outcome pricing is that we have to earn the next month of work by delivering the last one.

Q 01.11 What languages and regions do you support?

English, Mandarin, Japanese, Spanish, French, and German at full quality. Any other language on request — we'll be honest about where the AI is strong and where it still needs a human check. Clients live in 22 countries; our agents run in the region closest to your data.

Q 01.12 What if my team is nervous about "replacing jobs with AI"?

Fair worry. Our rollouts always start as co-pilot mode — the agent drafts, a human approves — before graduating to autonomous. We don't AI-automate anyone's job away on day one, and our change-management lead runs a session with your team the week before launch.

Pricing & billing

8 answers
Q 02.01 What counts as an "outcome"?

An outcome is one finished unit of work, defined in writing before we start: a triaged claim, a tagged lease, a drafted reply, a reconciled entry. If the AI finishes it within SLA and passes the agreed quality checks, we count it. If it doesn't, we don't bill.

Example "One outcome = one tenancy application reviewed, scored, and filed into PropertyTree. SLA: 12 minutes. Quality: 99% accuracy on a weekly 50-item QA sample."
Q 02.02 How much does a typical outcome cost?

Between A$0.40 and A$12, tiered by complexity. Simple classification or tagging is the low end; multi-step reasoning with document synthesis is the high end. The full rate card lives on the pricing page; your quote is fixed for the life of the contract.

Q 02.03 Are there any seat, user, or token fees?

No. Seats are unlimited. Tokens are our cost, not yours. Infrastructure pass-through is zero. You pay for finished outcomes and nothing else — the only line item on your invoice.

Q 02.04 How does billing work and when do I pay?

Invoices go out on the 1st for the previous month's SLA-passing outcomes. Net-14 terms by default, Net-30 on request. Pay by bank transfer, direct debit, or corporate card. Every outcome is line-itemed in the ops console so your finance team can audit before signing off.

Q 02.05 What if the AI processes something but we reject the output?

Rejections aren't billable. If your reviewer flags an outcome as wrong within 5 business days, we issue an automatic credit and the agent retrains against that failure. The AI automation has to be right and accepted to count.

Q 02.06 Is there a volume discount?

Yes — up to 35% off the per-outcome rate when you move to a hybrid plan with a small monthly base. Most teams cross that threshold around 20,000 outcomes/month. Your quote is re-optimised every quarter so you always sit on the best tier you qualify for.

Q 02.07 Can I cap my monthly spend?

Yes. Set a soft cap (we notify at 80% and 100%) or a hard cap (the agent pauses once hit). Caps are configured in the ops console under Billing → Guardrails and take effect immediately.

Q 02.08 Do you charge for set-up or onboarding?

The audit is free. Build-out fees apply only for custom connectors or deeply bespoke agents (usually A$4k–A$12k, disclosed before signing). Standard vertical agents with standard integrations have zero set-up fee — you start paying when the first SLA-passing outcome ships.

— 03 ·

AI agents

15 answers
Q 03.01 What exactly is an "AI agent" in Autopilot Labs terms?

A scoped worker. Each agent does one class of job — triage this, draft that, reconcile those — with a defined input, defined output, and a measurable SLA. Think of it as a new hire with a one-line job description and a perfect memory. Nothing about our agents is "general purpose."

Q 03.02 Which models power your agents?

A mix. We route each step to the model that gets it right cheapest — frontier models for reasoning, smaller models for classification, open-weight models where privacy demands it. The orchestration layer is ours; the model choice is not something you need to manage.

Q 03.03 Can I stop using Autopilot Labs and keep the AI agents?

No — but you own every input and every output. The orchestration, prompts, and eval harness stay with us, which is fair since we built and maintain them. If you leave, we export the last 12 months of outputs in JSON or CSV and you're free to rebuild elsewhere.

In practice no client has left in two years. If the AI automation stops earning its keep, we re-price or pause it — not hostage it.

Q 03.04 What if the agent doesn't know the answer?

It escalates. Every agent has a confidence threshold; below it, the work is routed to a human reviewer on your team (or ours, if you've opted in to managed review). Silent failure is the one thing we refuse to ship.

Q 03.05 How do you measure whether an agent is actually accurate?

Three layers, in the contract from day one:

  • A gold-set eval run on every prompt change (pre-deploy)
  • A random 50-item weekly QA sample, reviewed by humans
  • Your own reject/accept signal on each outcome in the ops console

If the rolling accuracy dips below the contracted threshold, we pause the agent and investigate before resuming.

Q 03.06 Can one agent work across multiple teams or departments?

Yes. Agents scope by workflow, not by team. If three departments share the same intake process, one agent handles all of them — with per-team routing, branding, and reporting views in the console.

Q 03.07 How fast is an agent? What's the latency?

Most outcomes ship in under 30 seconds. Document-heavy tasks (multi-page discovery, long claims) sit in the 2–5 minute band. Real-time chat-style agents respond in under 2 seconds for the first token. Latency is part of your SLA, not an afterthought.

Q 03.08 Can the agent learn from our own documents and past work?

Yes. Every agent is grounded in your SOPs, playbooks, templates, and a running index of previously approved outputs. We use retrieval and fine-tuning where it adds real accuracy — not as a buzzword. Your data never trains a shared model.

Q 03.09 What happens when you want to update an agent's behaviour?

Every change runs against the gold-set eval first. If accuracy holds, we ship to a 5% canary slice, then 25%, then 100% over 48 hours. You see every deploy in the console changelog and can roll back in one click.

Q 03.10 How many agents can I run at once?

As many as your workflows justify. Our largest client runs 27 in production across three continents. A typical mid-market team runs 3 to 6. There's no per-agent licence fee — add one whenever it pays for itself.

Q 03.11 Can I talk to the agent, or is it purely behind-the-scenes?

Both. Most agents run as background workers on a queue. Some are exposed as chat or Slack bots for your team to query. The decision is yours — we'll recommend whichever pattern fits the job.

Q 03.12 Can the agents trigger other systems (write-back, not just read)?

Yes. Write-back is the whole point. Agents post to Salesforce, draft in Outlook, file in SharePoint, update HubSpot, push to Xero, and so on. Every write is logged, reversible, and bounded by per-agent permissions you set.

Q 03.13 Do agents handle edge cases or just the happy path?

We scope the happy path first because that's where the volume is — 80% of cases usually. Everything outside that scope escalates to a human. Over time, recurring escalations become new scope items, priced and added to the agent.

Q 03.14 What if the underlying AI model changes or is deprecated?

Our problem, not yours. We re-eval on any model change and swap underlying providers without you noticing. You pay per outcome, not per token — so the model choice is entirely our optimisation to make.

Q 03.15 Can I A/B test two versions of an agent?

Yes. Split traffic by percentage or by segment (region, brand, case type). The ops console shows accuracy, latency, and cost per arm so you can pick the winner with data instead of vibes.

Security & data

9 answers
Q 04.01 Is my data used to train the AI?

Never by default. Your data is used only for your own agents, to improve your outputs, and is never used to train a shared or public model. Our model providers are contractually bound to the same — we publish the DPAs on request.

Q 04.02 Where is my data hosted?

By default, in the region nearest your business — AU, EU, UK, US, or Singapore. Residency is configurable per agent. Regulated clients (healthcare, legal, finance) can pin all data to a single region with full audit logs for compliance teams.

Q 04.03 What compliance certifications does Autopilot Labs hold?

SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR, the Australian Privacy Act, and HIPAA (for healthcare engagements). Our security overview and audit letters are in the trust centre — ask your account lead for access.

Q 04.04 How is PII handled when an agent reads our docs?

PII is detected, tagged, and scoped at ingress. Redaction happens before the prompt hits any model if your policy requires it. You choose the sensitivity tier per agent — we default to strict for healthcare, legal, and HR workflows.

Q 04.05 Can we run agents inside our own VPC?

Yes — single-tenant and BYOC (bring-your-own-cloud) deployments are available on the enterprise plan, on AWS, Azure, or GCP. We deploy and maintain, you own the control plane and the logs. Add-on fee applies; most clients don't need it.

Q 04.06 How long do you retain my data?

Default retention is 90 days for intermediate traces, 12 months for final outputs, and zero retention on any field you mark sensitive. You can tighten those to zero at the workflow level. Deletion is verified with a cryptographic receipt.

Q 04.07 Who at Autopilot Labs can see my data?

Only your named delivery team (typically 2–4 engineers) and our on-call SRE, under time-bounded, logged access. Every view is audited. Break-glass access for support requires two-person approval and expires in 24 hours.

Q 04.08 How do you prevent prompt injection and data leaks?

Input sanitisation, instruction-hierarchy prompts, tool-use allowlists, and output classifiers on every agent. We run a monthly red-team exercise against every production agent and publish the results to you in the compliance report.

Q 04.09 What happens to my data if I cancel?

You get a final export within 7 days. Everything is deleted within 30, with a signed deletion certificate. No "we keep it for marketing" clauses — if you leave, you leave clean.

Integrations

11 answers
Q 05.01 Which systems do you integrate with out of the box?

Around 140 connectors, covering the systems our thirteen verticals actually use — Salesforce, HubSpot, Outlook, Gmail, Slack, Teams, Xero, MYOB, Epic, Cerner, Relativity, iManage, Shopify, Magento, Workday, BambooHR, SAP, NetSuite, Jira, Linear, and dozens more. Full list on the integrations page.

Q 05.02 What if my system isn't on the list?

If it has an API or a database, we build a custom connector. Typical build is 3–8 days at a flat rate. For truly obscure or legacy systems, we can drive them via RPA or email as a fallback — less elegant, but it still lets us AI-automate the workflow.

Q 05.03 Do you work with on-premise or air-gapped systems?

Yes. We ship a lightweight on-prem runner that sits inside your network, holds the credentials, and talks outbound-only to our control plane. Nothing from us reaches your systems directly.

Q 05.04 How are credentials managed?

OAuth where possible, service accounts otherwise, all stored in a FIPS-140-2 vault per tenant with per-agent scopes. Nothing lands on engineering laptops. Credential rotation is automatic on a 90-day cycle or on demand.

Q 05.05 Can I trigger an agent from a webhook or button?

Yes. Every agent has a unique webhook endpoint and a signed HMAC payload contract. Trigger it from Zapier, n8n, a Salesforce flow, a Slack button, or your own service. Example payload:

POST https://api.autopilotlabs.io/v1/agents/intake-triage/run Authorization: Bearer <token> Content-Type: application/json { "case_id": "CASE-0042", "priority": "normal" }
Q 05.06 Do you have a public API?

Yes — REST + webhooks, OpenAPI 3.1 spec, SDKs in TypeScript, Python, and Go. Full docs at docs.autopilotlabs.io. You'll need an API key (issued in the ops console) and a signed integration agreement.

Q 05.07 Can agents send emails or messages directly?

Yes. Outbound email via your domain (we authenticate SPF/DKIM/DMARC during onboarding), Slack/Teams via official bots, and SMS via your existing provider. Every outbound message is logged and capped by a sending policy.

Q 05.08 What about SSO and user provisioning?

SAML, OIDC, and SCIM are standard. We support Okta, Azure AD/Entra, Google Workspace, JumpCloud, OneLogin, and PingIdentity. Role-based access is configurable down to the agent level.

Q 05.09 Can I stream outcomes to my data warehouse?

Yes. We stream to Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, or Databricks via Kafka, Fivetran, or native connectors. Daily CSV drops into S3, GCS, or Azure Blob if that suits your pipeline better.

Q 05.10 How do agents handle attachments and documents?

PDF, DOCX, XLSX, images (via OCR), scanned faxes, and audio (via transcription) are first-class inputs. Files are chunked, indexed, and cited in the final output so a human reviewer can trace a decision back to the source page.

Q 05.11 What if one of my integrations is down?

Agents retry with exponential backoff for 30 minutes, then park the job on a dead-letter queue and alert the on-call. Outcomes blocked by third-party outages don't count against your SLA — downtime on our side or the integration's side is excluded by the contract.

SLA & credit-back

6 answers
Q 06.01 What happens if the AI misses the SLA?

You don't pay for the missed outcome. If the rolling monthly SLA dips below contract, you also get a credit-back on the per-outcome rate, banded by severity. It's automatic — you don't have to file a claim, the console does it.

Credit-back bands ≥99.0% SLA → standard rate · 98.0–98.9% → 10% credit · 95.0–97.9% → 25% credit · below 95% → 50% credit and a written root-cause report within 5 business days.
Q 06.02 What is your target SLA?

99.1% on-time across the per-task plan and 99.3% on hybrid — rolling 90 days. Our actual measured number sits at 99.4% across all thirteen verticals as of Q2 2026. Each vertical has a per-agent target defined in the contract.

Q 06.03 What's considered "on time"?

Each agent has a per-outcome latency target in its contract, defined in minutes or seconds. An outcome is on time if it finishes under that target and passes quality checks. Both conditions must hold; a fast wrong answer doesn't count.

Q 06.04 How is the SLA calculated and reported?

(Outcomes that finished on time AND passed QA) ÷ (All eligible outcomes in the window), rolling 90 days. The ops console shows it live for your team and emails a monthly PDF summary to your finance contact.

Q 06.05 What counts as "not our fault" for the SLA?

A short, written list in the contract — third-party outages for systems you own, network loss at your office, data delivered in a format outside the agreed schema, or anything caused by an input your team rejected. Everything else is on us.

Q 06.06 Can I tighten the SLA if my business needs faster turnaround?

Yes. Per-agent SLA tiers are negotiable — tighter latency, higher QA sampling, priority routing. Premium tiers carry a small rate uplift but no platform fee. Most regulated clients (healthcare, legal) opt in for the tighter tiers.
