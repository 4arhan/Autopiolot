Cover Essay · Q2 2026

Why per-outcome
pricing beats
per-seat.

A seat tells you nothing about the work that was done. A reconciliation, a filed claim, a closed ticket — these are the units a business actually buys. We argue it is time to price agents the way accountants price audits.

PO
Priya Okonkwo Principal Economist · Autopilot Labs
14 min read
Apr 14, 2026
The per-seat SaaS model was an accident of accounting. It was not designed; it was inherited — from the days when a licence belonged to a human who sat in front of a screen. Agents do not sit. They do not take holidays, they do not forget how to use the tool, they do not churn because their manager left. Pricing them per seat is an act of nostalgia dressed as strategy, and the buyers are starting to notice. In this essay we walk through three live Autopilot Labs engagements where replacing seats with outcomes changed not just the commercial terms, but the shape of the work itself — and, in one case, the shape of the team that bought it.
