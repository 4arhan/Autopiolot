Section I · What we believe 01
Why Autopilot Labs

Four things we mean when we
say we ship finished work.

These aren't posters on a wall. They show up in every contract, every standup, every candidate's first week. Read them as a self-test: if any one of them makes you uncomfortable, we're probably not the company for you.

— 01 · Billing

We sell finished work, not hours.

Our clients pay per outcome shipped — a triaged claim, an abstracted lease, a closed-book entry. That discipline runs inside the company too. We measure what got out the door, not who looked busy doing it. Nobody here is paid for effort theatre.

— 02 · Clarity

Every commitment is in writing.

Every role has a written scope. Every project has an SLA. Every raise has a written threshold. If your manager made you a promise and it isn't in a doc, it didn't happen. We'd rather over-document than leave anyone guessing what good looks like.

— 03 · Honesty

No theatre — we turn down work that won't ship.

If an audit turns up a workflow we can't automate well, we say so and we don't book it. The same applies to engineering: if a project is doomed, the senior who flags it gets thanked, not punished. Killing bad work is as valued as finishing good work.

— 04 · Rigour

Boring is a feature.

Our agents run overnight without a human. That means migrations, retries, audit logs, observability, and write-ups. If you find boring work demeaning, you won't enjoy this place. The best engineers we've hired get obsessive about the reliability work nobody sees.

03.
Benefits · plainly

What we actually offer.

No perk theatre. No catered-lunch nonsense to paper over a broken schedule. Eight things we pay for, in writing, from day one — because they're the things we'd want if we were deciding whether to take the offer.

— 01 · Pay
Transparent comp bands

The band you see on the job page is the band we use internally. New hires land inside it. Annual reviews move you inside it. If we want to move the band, we move it for everyone at once.

— 02 · Time
4-day delivery weeks

Fridays are for deep work, admin, or off. No standing Friday meetings. No "quick syncs." We measure shipped work per person, per month — and the 4-day teams ship more than the 5-day ones did.

— 03 · Learning
A$8k annual learning

Courses, books, conferences, coaching — anything with a receipt and a write-up. Unused budget rolls over once. Your manager approves; we will never second-guess a reasonable request.

— 04 · Health
Full psych support

We cover a mental-health provider of your choice, up to 24 sessions a year, with no manager notification. Physical health: private cover for you and your immediate family, dental included.

— 05 · Family
Parental leave

24 weeks full pay primary · 12 weeks full pay secondary, regardless of gender or how your family is formed. Phased return over 8 weeks. No surprise reorgs while you're out — in writing.

— 06 · Rest
Sabbatical at year 3

Six weeks fully paid after three years, every three years. Not "unlimited" leave theatre — a hard-enforced number we fund with a line item. Most take it as one block; a few split it.

— 07 · Ownership
Equity for all

Every full-time employee gets a meaningful grant on day one. We publish internally how the pool is distributed by role and level. Double-trigger acceleration in any change of control.

— 08 · Where
Remote-equal culture

Three of our seven leads are remote. Decisions are made in docs, not hallways. Melbourne HQ is optional for most roles and required for a few — we say which, clearly, on every posting.

§ 05 — Honest filters Read before applying

We probably won't hire
you if …

— 01

… you want to build AGI.

Autopilot Labs is an agency. We build agents that triage claims and abstract leases. If your north star is a frontier-lab problem, you'll be unhappy here — and honestly, we'd rather you go do that.

— 02

… you want to manage a 40-person team.

We have about 38 people. We plan to have about 60 by the end of next year and no more. Almost every senior role is player-coach. If your career goal is a wide org chart, we're not the move.

— 03

… you hate writing.

Every decision, scope, and commitment here is in writing. PRs have essays. Specs have acceptance tests. If your last good doc was a deck with 4 bullets per slide, the first month will be painful.

— 04

… you can't turn down clients.

We decline roughly one in four audits because the workflow won't ship well. That muscle is also the one that keeps our on-SLA number above 99%. If "revenue now, quality later" is your reflex, this won't work.
