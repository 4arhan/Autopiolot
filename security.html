How we keep
your data safe
while AI does the work.

◆ Working architecture · not a whitepaper Last reviewed · 02 April 2026 ● Live — every layer documented

An AI agent that finishes the job has to read data it was never meant to keep. This page documents every layer of the system — from the browser tab to the model weights — so your CISO can read it in one sitting and sign off in two. No vague posture statements. No rebadged vendor logos.

Autopilot Labs ships vertical AI agents into regulated industries — healthcare, legal, finance. The security model assumes the agent will be asked to do something stupid or dangerous every hour of every day, and must refuse, log, and alert. Humans stay in the loop on irreversible work. Every action is signed, replayable, and revokable.

Scope of this document Applies to all Autopilot Labs production workloads operated from the Melbourne (au-east-1) and Sydney (au-east-2) regions, plus the Frankfurt (eu-central-1) enclave for EU customers. US tenants are routed through us-east-1 via dedicated VPC peering. Does not cover our public marketing site.

Section II · Principles 02
Six things we never compromise

What the architecture
is built to protect.

An AI agent that can ship 2.4M documents in a weekend can also set 2.4M documents on fire in a weekend. These are the six non-negotiables that decide what the agent may, and may not, do.

— 01 · Access

Least privilege
by default.

Every agent starts with zero scopes. Scopes are granted per-workflow, expire in ≤15 minutes, and every privileged call is signed and logged. The read-only bot cannot, as a matter of code, write.

Enforced at · policy decision point
— 02 · Isolation

Tenant-isolated models.

Your documents never train another customer's agent. Fine-tunes, RAG indexes, and context windows are keyed to your tenant ID and cannot be read across the boundary. No shared embeddings. No shared prompt caches.

Enforced at · model loader · vector store
— 03 · Control

Human-in-the-loop on
irreversible actions.

Anything that can't be undone in 60 seconds — sending email, filing with a regulator, moving money, closing a ticket — requires a human approval. The default answer is ask, not assume.

Enforced at · tool broker · OOB channel
— 04 · Revocation

Kill-switch on
every agent.

Any operator on your side, and any Autopilot Labs on-call engineer, can freeze a single agent — or the whole fleet — in under three seconds. Freeze halts in-flight work cleanly and writes a freeze record to the audit ledger.

Enforced at · runtime scheduler
— 05 · Forensics

Signed audit log.

Every prompt, every tool call, every output, every approval is written append-only, signed with per-tenant keys, and sealed weekly. You can replay any agent's decisions to the exact byte, seven years back.

Enforced at · audit ledger · WORM
— 06 · Retention

Zero-retention mode
available.

For regulated workloads, the agent runs with no prompt caching, no embedding storage, and no inference logs. Input is destroyed at 10 minutes; only the outcome, hash, and audit record persist. Enabled per agent, per contract.

Enforced at · runtime · ledger

Section IV · AI-specific controls 04
What's unique to agent systems

Controls you don't
find in a SaaS SOC 2.

Classical web security stops at the gateway. An AI agent can be talked into betraying its own instructions in plain English — so the rest of the perimeter lives inside the model, the prompt, the tool call, and the output. These are the controls we run specifically for that.

AI-01

Prompt-injection defense

User content is framed with structural delimiters and a deterministic parser verifies the agent never confuses instruction with input. Suspicious patterns (role-change, exfiltration, scope escalation) are blocked at the pre-inference filter and flagged on the ledger.

Ref OWASP LLM01 · 2023 · revised Mar 2026
AI-02

Output sanitization

Every model output passes a post-inference filter that strips executable content, redacts PII not authorised for the destination, and runs a schema check. Outputs that fail schema never reach the downstream tool — they are held for human review.

Ref OWASP LLM02 · insecure output handling
AI-03

Deterministic mode toggle

For workflows where the same input must produce the same output — insurance pricing, financial reconciliation, medical coding — the agent runs with temperature 0, seed pinned, tool set frozen, and a content-hash assertion. Drift between runs triggers an incident.

Ref internal SOP · DET-V2.3
AI-04

Citation requirements

Any assertion of fact in an agent output must carry a citation to a source document in the tenant's corpus. Uncited assertions are held and flagged. The ops console shows the exact line of the source doc the agent was reading when it wrote the sentence.

Ref OWASP LLM09 · overreliance
AI-05

Hallucination flagging

A second model scores the primary output for internal consistency and source-grounding. Below a per-workflow threshold, the outcome is quarantined and routed to the human-in-the-loop queue. We publish the false-positive and miss rates monthly.

Ref OWASP LLM09 · internal metric · HAL-S
AI-06

Tool-call scope minimisation

The tool broker ships the smallest surface area the agent can possibly need for the current step. A contract-review agent that never sends email is not given send-email — even if another customer's contract-review agent uses it. Scopes are enumerated per workflow.

Ref OWASP LLM08 · excessive agency
AI-07

Training-data isolation

Your data never enters a shared training run. Fine-tunes are per-tenant and live in a per-tenant namespace. We do not use production traffic for model improvement unless explicitly opted-in — and even then, only the user's own tenant benefits.

Ref OWASP LLM03 · training data poisoning
AI-08

Model supply-chain integrity

Model weights are pinned by SHA, signed at ingestion, and loaded from a read-only store. We track provenance for every model in production, including the training set license status and any vendor security advisories.

Ref OWASP LLM05 · supply chain

Section VIII · Data lifecycle 08
From ingest to destruction

Every byte has
a documented exit.

Data lifecycle is the one control most AI vendors quietly skip. We publish ours. Below: the six states a customer record moves through, the controls at each, and the SLA for deletion on request.

01 · Ingest
Received at edge
Client delivers via TLS 1.3 to the tenant-scoped API. Payload is validated, schema-checked, and tagged with tenant and classification.
TLS 1.3 · schema · class tag
02 · Route
Policy-checked
ABAC evaluation decides if this tenant, this workflow, this key can ingest this class of data. Failures rejected before any persistence.
PDP · tenant scope
03 · Persist
Written at rest
AES-256-GCM, per-tenant CMK. Written to per-tenant schema. Vector embeddings keyed to tenant. Access logged to audit ledger.
AES-256 · CMK · ledger
04 · Process
Read by agent
Ephemeral sandbox reads the record, infers, writes outcome. No residual copy is held in the sandbox — process memory is zeroed on exit.
gVisor · memzero · no-log
05 · Retain
Per contract window
Retention per contract (default 90 days for embeddings, 7 years for audit). Early-expire button available in the ops console.
TTL · lifecycle policy
06 · Destroy
Verified deletion
Crypto-shred by CMK revocation plus overwrite on primary. Deletion receipt signed and delivered to the tenant. 30-day SLA on DSAR.

Section IX · FAQ 09
Questions the procurement team will ask

Answers before
the questionnaire lands.

Twelve questions we get on every security review. If yours isn't below, email security@autopilotlabs.io and you'll have a written reply inside one business day.

Q-01 Does Autopilot Labs train models on my data?
No. Your data never enters a shared training run. If you explicitly opt in to per-tenant fine-tuning, the resulting model lives only in your tenant namespace and is used only for your workflows. Opt-out is the default and requires a written signature to reverse.
Q-02 Where is my data physically stored?
By default, in the region selected at contract time: Melbourne (au-east-1), Sydney (au-east-2), Frankfurt (eu-central-1), or N. Virginia (us-east-1). Data does not leave the selected region except for cross-region backups, which stay inside the same legal jurisdiction.
Q-03 Who at Autopilot Labs can see my data?
Engineers on the incident response rotation, and only after break-glass access is approved by two senior officers. Every such access is logged to the tenant audit ledger, visible to you, and reviewed weekly. No customer-support engineer has standing read access to tenant data.
Q-04 Do you have SOC 2 / ISO 27001?
Yes. SOC 2 Type II (most recent report: March 2026) and ISO 27001 + ISO 27701 (issued November 2025). Both reports are available under NDA. Section 07 above lists every framework and status.
Q-05 What happens if an agent goes rogue?
In order: the hallucination scorer quarantines the outcome, the tool broker refuses the next call, the kill-switch is pulled (either automatically on a policy trigger or by an on-call engineer), and the ledger is sealed for forensics. Time from anomaly detection to halt, median: 4.2 seconds.
Q-06 Can I use my own encryption keys?
Yes, on Enterprise contracts. BYOK is implemented via AWS KMS external key store or Vault transit. You keep the master and rotate on your schedule. Revoke the master and we lose access to your data inside a replication window (under 15 minutes).
Q-07 What's your incident notification SLA?
72 hours from confirmed breach of confidentiality, integrity, or availability of customer data. For incidents that do not touch customer data, the window is 7 days or the next monthly bulletin, whichever is sooner. Notifications come by email and by a signed entry on status.autopilotlabs.io.
Q-08 Do you support SSO / SCIM?
Yes. SAML 2.0 and OIDC for SSO on every plan. SCIM 2.0 for provisioning on Growth and above. Full list of supported IdPs: Okta, Azure AD / Entra, Google Workspace, JumpCloud, OneLogin, Ping, Auth0, Duo, Keycloak.
Q-09 What's in your subprocessor list?
AWS (hosting), Cloudflare (edge + WAF), Anthropic / OpenAI / fine-tuned OSS (inference), Datadog (logs and metrics), Stripe (billing), Linear (issue tracking). Full list with regions and purpose lives at /subprocessors. Thirty-day notice on any change.
Q-10 Can you delete all my data?
Yes. 30 days from written request, end-to-end. Crypto-shred of CMK is performed inside the first hour; physical overwrite on primary storage completes inside the first week; backups age out on the standard 30-day rotation. A signed deletion receipt is delivered to the tenant owner.
Q-11 Do you test for prompt injection?
Yes, continuously. Our internal red team maintains a corpus of 4,800+ prompt-injection cases (including multi-turn, tool-smuggled, and document-embedded). Every model version is evaluated against the corpus on release and nightly thereafter. Regression drops the build.
Q-12 What if I need something stricter than the defaults?
Enterprise contracts support: single-tenant VPC, dedicated inference pool, customer-operated key management, zero-retention mode, on-prem connector for sensitive corpora, and an annotation of your internal classification labels onto our audit ledger. Talk to us.
