Section II · Principles 02
What we refuse to do

Eight articles we've
signed our name to.

These aren't marketing. They're the clauses in our contracts — and the reasons we've turned down engagements that would have been easy revenue.

The Autopilot Labs articles.

We sign these into every statement of work. If we ever violate one, the engagement terminates that day and every outcome billed in the breach period is refunded.

I.

No seat-based billing.

Ever. You pay for finished work, or you pay nothing. Seats punish adoption; we want adoption.

II.

No token pass-through.

Inference cost is our problem. If our model gets cheaper, we keep the margin — and pass it on at the next quarterly rate review, in writing.

III.

We turn work down.

If the audit shows the workflow isn't ripe — not enough volume, too much judgement, bad data — we say so and we walk. Roughly a third of audits end this way.

IV.

Humans own the final click.

For every regulated or high-blast-radius workflow, a human approves before anything irreversible. We make that click fast, not absent.

V.

SLA misses refund automatically.

No support ticket. No negotiation. Missed outcomes credit back on the next invoice without you asking.

VI.

Your data leaves when you leave.

Weights trained on your corpus are yours. On churn, we hand over weights, logs, and export artefacts within 30 days — and purge our side.

VII.

We don't sell AGI.

We sell finished tasks in your industry. If the job needs cross-vertical general reasoning, we're honestly not the shop.

VIII.

Plain English, always.

If a clause in our contract needs a lawyer to translate, we rewrite it. Your ops team should be able to audit our SLA without legal on the call.
